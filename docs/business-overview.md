# Judgment over tooling.

An AI advisory practice for operator-led companies. ACE doesn't sell a platform. It helps leadership teams make good AI decisions, frames AI work as Rocks so it actually ships, and builds the few things actually worth building.

## Positioning

Between the AI vendors knocking on the door and the team pressure to "do something about AI," most operators make either the worst decision — buy everything — or the second-worst — buy nothing. ACE provides a third option: a clear framework, a trusted outside voice, and hands when needed.

**The core promise:** I don't build AI for you unless I have to. I help your leadership team make good AI decisions, then either configure existing tools to solve the problem (most cases) or build something custom (when nothing off the shelf will work).

### Where the value lives

Better decisions on which AI initiatives to pursue. Lower spend on the ones that don't fit. Real, working AI inside the workflows where it earns its keep. Three outcomes, repeatable across every engagement.

### The new lever

AI changes the investment-to-output curve. Some companies will get more output for the same investment. The smarter ones will get the same output for less investment. Both are valid plays — but the leadership team needs the framework to know which one they're running.

## How AI Fits Inside EOS

The wrong frame: "build AI tools for each EOS component." The right frame: **work within the EOS framework you already have.** Terminology matters here. AI work that isn't framed as a Rock gets forgotten, deprioritized, or abandoned. The whole engagement is built on translating AI initiatives into the language and rhythms the leadership team already uses.

### The six components, with an AI lens

#### 01 · Vision — AI as a filter

Add an AI lens to the existing 3-year picture and 1-year plan. For every goal, ask whether AI makes it 10x better, marginally better, or worse-if-wrong. Output: a one-page "AI Horizon" appended to the V/TO, revisited quarterly.

#### 02 · People — Seats & accountability

Define an AI-accountable seat on the Accountability Chart — usually bolted to an existing role, not a new hire. Sponsor (Integrator) → Operator (department lead) → Builder (vendor) → Reviewer (ACE). Plus baseline AI literacy for everyone else; not everyone needs to own AI, but everyone needs willingness to adapt.

#### 03 · Data — The four-question filter

A decision framework the team runs themselves on any AI pitch. Most pitches fail at least one question. The ones that pass all four are worth piloting. This is the signature IP of the practice.

#### 04 · Issues — Risk, cost, security, and AI candidates

AI failures, cost overruns, and security concerns belong on the existing Issues list. So do AI *opportunities*. Common candidates worth bringing forward: proposal generation, customer feedback collection, employee feedback, market data gathering. Cost discipline: monthly burn caps. Security basics: business-tier accounts, no PII in consumer tools, vendor due diligence. Shadow AI is the real threat.

#### 05 · Process — AI in the loop

AI doesn't replace a process — it lives inside one. For every AI-touched workflow: trigger, AI's job, human checkpoint, fallback, kill switch. Documented like any other SOP.

#### 06 · Traction — AI as Rocks

Every AI initiative becomes a Rock — named owner, binary done definition, sub-$15K budget ceiling, explicit kill criterion. 90 days. Ship or stop. **If it isn't a Rock, it doesn't happen.** ACE attends quarterly Rock-setting, mid-quarter check-ins, and post-mortems.

## The Decision Filter

Before any AI project leaves the idea stage, it runs through four questions. The framework is taught to the leadership team so they can run it themselves on every future pitch.

1. **Is it a rules, data, or judgment problem?** Rules → write rules, no AI. Data lookup → dashboard. Judgment at scale → AI candidate.
2. **Do we have the data to support it?** If it needs three years of clean data and yours is in a filing cabinet, the project is actually "fix our data first."
3. **What's the cost of being wrong?** Low stakes + high volume → AI candidate. High stakes + low volume → human call. Never automate the customer-trust layer.
4. **What would we do with the answer?** If there's no clear action following the output, don't build it. Insight without action is noise.

*Note on positioning vs. existing frameworks:* the EOS conference's 90-day AI implementation framework (Success.co) maps 1:1 to standard project management. The four-question filter adds what's actually missing — whether you should run the project at all.

## Engagement Tiers — Three Ways In

Three tiers, named for the acronym: Advise, Coach, Execute. Each tier represents a different depth of involvement and a different *kind* of work.

### The shape of each tier

| Tier | Type of work | Output | Pricing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Advise** | Pure thinking. No tools touched, no implementation. | Decisions, frameworks, recommendations. | ~$1.2–1.5K/mo · ~2 hrs/wk |
| **Coach** | Configuring existing tools. Picking the right Lego pieces and snapping them together. | A working solution using off-the-shelf tools — Claude Projects, GPT custom assistants, no-code automations, vendor SaaS configured to fit. | ~$3K/mo · ~5 hrs/wk |
| **Execute** | Building the Lego piece that doesn't exist yet. Real engineering on a novel problem. | A hosted custom application or pipeline that solves something no off-the-shelf tool can. | $10–40K per project, fixed-fee |

**The clarifying line:** Coach picks the right Lego pieces and snaps them together. Execute builds the new piece that doesn't exist yet.

### Tier 01 · Advise — "just talk"

*Outside thinking, no implementation.*

**~$1.2–1.5K per month** — anchored on ~2 hours/week at $150/hr

- AI 101 / literacy sessions for the leadership team
- Help thinking through Rocks before they're scoped
- Vendor pitch reviews as needed
- Quarterly attendance at offsite for AI level-set
- No tools touched, no implementation, no hands-on building

*Best fit when:* there's no immediate AI project, but pressure is building from vendors, team, or competitors. Lowest-friction entry point. Most clients start here.

### Tier 02 · Coach — hold-your-hand implementation

*Helping the team get small AI wins using tools that already exist.*

**~$3K per month** — anchored on ~5 hours/week at $150/hr

The work here is not engineering. It's **tool literacy plus judgment**: identifying small initiatives, evaluating which existing tools fit, configuring them properly, and walking the team through using them. Most operator-led AI problems are solvable this way without a single line of custom code.

- Identifying and scoping small AI initiatives the team can actually finish
- Evaluating existing tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, vendor SaaS) for fit
- Configuring tools to solve the specific problem — building Claude Projects with company PDFs and instructions, setting up custom GPTs, wiring up automation tools
- Documenting how the configured tool fits into the existing workflow (trigger, AI's job, human checkpoint, fallback, kill switch)
- Training the team on the configured solution and handing it off
- Attending leadership meetings and offsites; coaching ongoing AI judgment
- Coordinating Execute engagements when a problem outgrows off-the-shelf tools

*Example Coach work:*

- **Knowledge assistant.** A Claude Project loaded with company SOPs, contracts, and policies plus instructions, so the leadership team can ask questions about their own documentation. No code. Two hours of setup. Ongoing care from Coach as docs change.
- **Proposal first-drafter.** A Custom GPT trained on past winning proposals plus a structured prompt, used to generate initial drafts that humans edit. Built in an afternoon.
- **Customer feedback synthesizer.** A weekly workflow where support tickets get exported, dropped into Claude with a structured prompt, and themes get summarized for the leadership review. No new infrastructure.

*Best fit when:* the team has AI Rocks identified but they're solvable with existing tools used well. The team needs someone who knows the tooling landscape and can shortcut their evaluation, configuration, and adoption.

### Tier 03 · Execute — build the new thing

*Real engineering work on a problem that off-the-shelf tools can't solve.*

**$10–40K per project** — fixed-fee, anchored on $150/hr × scoped hours

This tier is where ACE writes code, designs systems, and hosts what gets built. The defining test: **does the problem require something novel that no existing tool can do?** If yes, Execute. If a Claude Project or vendor SaaS could do 80% of it, that's Coach work.

- Scoped 6–12 week fixed-fee engagement
- Tied to a single AI Rock that requires custom engineering
- Real software development — APIs, agents, pipelines, data infrastructure, hosted applications
- Includes hosting, deployment, and operational handoff
- Concrete deliverable plus full handoff documentation
- Internal team training so they can use and maintain it

*Example Execute work:*

- **Custom GTM engine.** A system that pulls leads from CRM, enriches with multiple data sources, scores using a custom model trained on the client's historical wins, and writes back into the CRM with prioritization. Hosted, monitored, maintained.
- **Customer semantic data agent.** An agent that ingests customer interaction data across channels, builds a unified customer understanding, and answers natural-language questions from the leadership team about customer behavior. Custom build, hosted infrastructure.
- **Churn signal pipeline.** A data pipeline that monitors contract behavior in real time, applies a custom-trained churn model, and surfaces save-the-customer calls to the right account owner — integrated with the existing systems.
- **Auto-Scorecard system.** Custom integration with the client's specific stack (e.g. ServiceTitan + QBO) when no off-the-shelf tool covers the integration cleanly.

*Best fit when:* the team has a specific AI Rock that genuinely requires custom engineering — when "the right Lego pieces don't exist yet" or when the integration burden is too high for an off-the-shelf solution. Concurrent: 2–3 projects.
