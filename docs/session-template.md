# Decisions in the room.

A working session for leadership teams making AI decisions. Not a status meeting. Not a workshop. The kind of session where things get decided, owned, and occasionally killed — on purpose. AI work that doesn't become a Rock doesn't happen.

## Three Session Types — Pick the Right One

> **Roy's principle:** Mixing levels in one room risks circular conversation and limits strategic depth. Separate audiences for separate sessions.

| Session type | Audience | Length | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strategic Session** | Leadership team only (Visionary, Integrator, leaders on the Accountability Chart) | 90 min monthly, 3 hrs quarterly | Vision, prioritization, Rock setting and review |
| **Execution Session** | Leadership + department heads / frontline supervisors | 60–90 min, frequency per Rock | Translate Rocks into workflow changes; resolve operational AI issues |
| **AI 101 Session** | Broad org — anyone who'll touch AI in their work | 75 min, one-time or annual refresh | Vocabulary, vendor literacy, security basics, decision filter exposure |

*Note on naming:* "Execution Session" refers to the session type where workflow-level AI work gets reviewed. It is not the same as the "Execute" engagement tier, which is the scoped-build offering. The naming overlap is real but contextual — Strategic and Execution sessions can both be run inside any tier (Advise, Coach, or Execute).

This template covers the Strategic Session in detail. The Execution Session and AI 101 use the same opening AI Level Set (Segment 01) but diverge after that. AI 101 has its own dedicated outline.

## Strategic Session — Purpose

By the end of the session, the leadership team has decisions made — on which AI initiatives to pursue, which to kill, and who owns what — documented in a way that survives the week. The discipline of the session is what makes the framework valuable. If we drift, the next session is half as useful.

> **The shape of it:** Ninety minutes. One new decision. Nothing deferred to "study further" more than once.

### What the client brings to each session

- Any AI pitches received since the last session
- Current state of in-flight AI Rocks
- Issues or failures from the quarter
- One honest question about AI they haven't known who to ask

## Pre-work — 48 Hours Prior

Sent to each leader three days before. Completed in a shared doc the day before. Ten minutes of thought from each leader beats ninety minutes of fumbling in the room.

### 1. Inbox scan

What AI-related pitches, demos, or vendor emails have hit your desk this quarter? List them. One line each.

### 2. Shadow AI check

Where in your workflow have you (or your team) started using AI tools informally? ChatGPT for drafts, Claude for research, Copilot in code, AI notetakers in meetings? Be honest — this isn't a trap.

### 3. The one frustration

What's one repeatable task or decision in your seat that consistently costs you time or gets done inconsistently? Don't pre-filter for "is this AI?" — just name the frustration.

### 4. The gut-check question

What's the AI question you don't want to ask in front of your team because it feels too basic? Email it to ACE directly. We'll bring it up generically if it's a pattern across the team.

## The Agenda — 90 Minutes Total

### Segment 01 · AI Level-Set (5 min)

*The mandatory opener for every session, regardless of type.*

Quick reset on shared vocabulary. Even with the same team every month, AI moves fast and people drift. Cover one or two of:

- One thing that changed in the AI landscape this month worth knowing
- A piece of vocabulary that came up in last session that's worth defining
- A common misconception worth correcting before strategic discussion

Two to five minutes. Not a lecture. Not consulting slides. The point is: *before we make decisions about AI, we're talking about the same thing.*

### Segment 02 · Ground Check (5 min)

Temperature read, not a presentation. Each leader answers the same question in under a minute. No cross-talk, no responses. ACE captures on the board. Patterns emerge without prompting.

> **The prompt:** Since our last session, one thing about AI in our business that's working — and one thing that isn't.

### Segment 03 · Pitch Triage (20 min)

Walk through the vendor pitches surfaced in pre-work. Each one runs through the four-question filter live, on the board. Verdict gets made in the room — not taken under advisement.

1. Is it a rules, data, or judgment problem?
2. Do we have the data to support it?
3. What's the cost of being wrong?
4. What would we do with the answer?

Verdict for each: **✕ Skip** · **~ Reframe** · **✓ Pilot**

**If nothing was pre-surfaced**, run the same triage on common Issues-list candidates worth considering as AI work:

- Proposal generation
- Customer feedback collection & synthesis
- Employee feedback / pulse surveys
- Market data gathering

**Target:** 3–5 pitches in 20 minutes. A pitch that takes longer is either its own session or not a real candidate yet.

### Segment 04 · The Signal Block (15 min)

ACE brings one signal from outside the business. Topic rotates each quarter so context compounds without drowning the team.

- **Q1 — Cost & model landscape.** What did AI cost this quarter? What's changing?
- **Q2 — Security & shadow AI.** Actual risk profile and what peers are doing.
- **Q3 — Vertical signal.** What comparable companies are actually building.
- **Q4 — People & literacy.** Where AI literacy is drifting and what to do about it.

**Format:** 8 minutes from ACE, 7 minutes of Q&A. Not a lecture. Not consulting slides. One specific, useful thing the team wouldn't have read on their own.

### Segment 05 · Rock Check-In (20 min)

For every active AI Rock, the owner answers three questions in under four minutes:

1. **Where are you?** On track, at risk, or off track.
2. **What's in the way?** Named, specific. Not "bandwidth."
3. **Do you need the team?** Yes, and here's the decision I need — or no, I'm good.

If a Rock is off track, it runs through the kill criterion set at kickoff. Sunk-cost resistance gets pushed back on, in the room. If a Rock finishes mid-quarter: a five-minute post-mortem.

> **The Rocks rule:** If AI work isn't a Rock, it isn't happening. Side projects get forgotten. Initiatives get deprioritized. Only Rocks ship.

### Segment 06 · One New Decision (15 min)

Every session produces exactly one new decision — a new Rock scoped, a workflow change approved, or a named "we're not doing this" that closes a recurring distraction. If no decision emerges naturally, we don't manufacture one. The signal that the team isn't ready is useful in itself.

For any new AI Rock scoped, the one-pager is completed before anyone leaves the room:

| Field | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Rock statement** | Binary, measurable |
| **Owner** | Single seat — not a committee |
| **Budget ceiling** | Dollar cap |
| **Kill criterion** | The specific signal we stop on |
| **Week-6 checkpoint** | What we expect by mid-quarter |
| **Success definition** | What "done" looks like at 90 days |

### Segment 07 · Close (10 min)

**Confidential round (5 min).** Each leader, one sentence: *"Between now and next session, the thing I'm actually worried about is ___."* Not performance theater — the honest read that tells ACE what's coming next quarter. Held in confidence, not reported up.

**Action capture (5 min).** ACE reads back the decisions made, owners assigned, and kill criteria set. Any correction happens now — not in follow-up email.

## Execution Session — Variant Notes

Same opening (AI Level-Set). After that, the session diverges:

- Skip Segments 03 (pitch triage) and 04 (signal block) — those are leadership-level conversations
- Replace with: **workflow-level review** of any AI-touched processes and current friction
- Replace with: **tactical Rock execution** — what's blocking the people doing the work
- Keep Segments 06 (one decision) and 07 (close)

Best run quarterly or per-Rock-cadence, not monthly. Mixing leadership and execution voices in the same monthly meeting is what Roy explicitly cautioned against.

## AI 101 Session — Variant Notes

Different format entirely. Covered in the dedicated AI 101 outline. Use the AI Level-Set segment from this template as the opener, then follow the AI 101 agenda for the remaining 70 minutes.

## Post-Session Deliverable

Within 48 hours, ACE delivers a single page — not meeting minutes, but a working artifact the owner can forward to an advisor, investor, or successor and have them understand where the company is on AI in 90 seconds.

- Decisions made this session — three to seven bullets, maximum
- Updated AI Rock status across all active initiatives
- Pitches reviewed this session, with verdicts
- One signal from ACE heading into next quarter
- Open questions flagged for the next session

## What This Session Is Not

- A place for ACE to demo tools
- A place for vendor pitches
- A debate on whether "AI is hype"
- A therapy session for AI anxiety
- A place where decisions get deferred more than once
- A replacement for the team's weekly operating cadence
- A mixed-audience session — pick the right session type for the right people

## Cadence

- **Strategic — Monthly** — standard cadence for Coach-tier clients. 90 minutes, virtual or on-site.
- **Strategic — Quarterly extended (3 hours)** — embedded in the client's quarterly offsite. Sets AI Rocks for the coming quarter.
- **Execution — Per Rock cadence** — typically every 2–4 weeks during active Rock execution.
- **AI 101 — One-time or annual refresh** — onboarding the broad org, refreshing as the AI landscape shifts.

## Notes for Andrew

- Always open with the AI Level-Set — even with teams you've worked with for months. Vocabulary drifts faster than you think.
- If the leadership team and department heads are both present in a session, that's a signal to split the meeting next time. Note it in the deliverable.
- The "Rocks rule" reframing is the most important sentence in this template. Repeat it. Make it a refrain.
- Issues-list AI candidates (proposal generation, customer feedback, employee feedback, market data) are the safest first-AI-Rocks for clients with no prior AI work — pre-loaded suggestions when the team draws a blank.
