# Show, don't sell.

A first meeting with a prospective client. The purpose is not to solve their problems — it's to show them what ACE does, share examples of how similar operators have used us, and surface whether there's a real fit. By the end, both sides know whether to move forward.

## Format Options

Per Roy's feedback (Apr 25): the original 90-minute format is too short once a workshop component is included. Pick the format that matches the prospect.

| Format | When to use | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **90-min Discovery** | Cold or lukewarm prospects, exploratory conversations, no working component | Mutual fit assessment, recommended tier, next-step decision |
| **Half-day Discovery + Workshop** (8am–lunch or lunch–5pm) | Warm prospects ready to engage, leadership team available, want to feel the working session experience | Same as above, plus a working component (filter applied to one real pitch or one Rock scoping exercise) so they leave with a tangible deliverable |

**For Kelly (Schaefer) and Dave (Suburban Steel):** half-day format. Both are warm and the workshop component creates the first artifact of the engagement before pricing is even on the table.

## Designing the Agenda

**Roy's principle:** Define the desired outcome first, then build the agenda backward.

Before scheduling, decide what "successful end of meeting" looks like for this specific prospect. Three common outcomes:

- **Outcome A — Tier & engagement decided.** Prospect leaves with a clear tier recommendation and an agreed next step. Standard 90-min format.
- **Outcome B — One pitch or Rock filtered together.** Prospect experiences the framework live with their own example. Half-day format.
- **Outcome C — Leadership team level-set.** Multiple leaders need shared vocabulary before any work begins. Half-day with AI 101 component.

The agenda below covers Outcome A. Outcomes B and C extend with workshop blocks at the end.

## Purpose

This meeting is explicitly **not a working session**. We don't try to solve their AI problems live (unless half-day format with workshop block). We don't run their pitches through the filter for free. We don't draft a Rock with them. Those are paid engagements.

This meeting is for them to leave with three things:

1. A clear understanding of how ACE thinks about AI for operator-led companies
2. Three or four real examples of how that thinking has played out for similar businesses
3. An honest read on which engagement tier — if any — fits where they are

**The shape of it:** Listen first. Show the framework. Tell stories. Let them ask. Be honest about fit, including when it isn't.

## Light Pre-Meeting Prep

Sent to the prospect 48 hours before. Three lines, max. We don't want them to over-prepare — we want them to show up curious, not rehearsed.

- **What's prompting this conversation?** The AI vendor pitch, the team pressure, the curiosity, the specific problem — name it in a sentence.
- **Who's in the room?** Just the owner, or the leadership team? Both work, different conversations.
- **How do you currently run your business?** EOS, Scaling Up, no formal system — context shapes the conversation.

## The 90-Minute Agenda (Standard)

### Segment 01 · Welcome & Framing (5 min)

Open by setting expectations explicitly:

"Here's what this hour and a half is and isn't. It is: me showing you how ACE thinks about AI, sharing examples of how that's worked for companies like yours, and helping you figure out whether we're a fit. It isn't: me solving any of your AI problems today, or pitching you a contract by the end. We'll know by the end whether there's a next step. Sound good?"

This framing matters. Most prospects walk into a "discovery call" expecting to be sold to. Naming what this meeting is — and isn't — relaxes them and changes the energy.

### Segment 02 · Their World (15 min)

Listen first. Open with two questions and let them talk:

1. **"Tell me about the business."** Industry, size, who they serve, how they make money. Five minutes of context.
2. **"What's bringing AI into the conversation right now?"** Vendor pitches? Team pressure? A specific problem? Competitive anxiety? Listen for the underlying tension, not just the stated reason.

Take notes. Don't react. Don't pitch yet. The goal is to have enough context that the rest of the meeting feels tailored, not generic.

### Segment 03 · AI Level-Set (10 min)

*Per Roy's "always start with AI level-set" rule.* Even if the leaders nod when you say "AI," everyone has a different mental model of what that means. A short, plain-English level-set prevents 60 minutes of circular conversation later.

Cover three things:

- **What AI actually is** in operator terms — pattern recognition at scale, not magic
- **The two flavors operators see most** — generative (drafting, summarizing) and predictive (forecasting, classifying)
- **The most useful mental model** — AI is a fast, capable, occasionally confidently-wrong intern. Treat it like one.

Keep it conversational. Don't lecture. The point is to establish shared vocabulary before going strategic.

### Segment 04 · How ACE Thinks About AI (10 min)

Walk through the positioning and the four-question filter. Keep it conversational — this is not a deck pitch.

The core arc:

- **Judgment over tooling.** Most vendors are selling tools. Most consultants are selling tools. Operators don't need more tools — they need the discipline to evaluate which AI investments will pay off, the engineering to ship the ones that do, and the honesty to walk away from the ones that won't.
- **What that means for your business.** Better decisions on which initiatives to pursue. Lower spend on the ones that don't fit. Real, working AI inside the workflows where it earns its keep.
- **How ACE delivers it.** Three tiers depending on depth needed — Advise, Coach, Execute. AI work always becomes a Rock so it actually ships.
- **The four-question filter.** Walk them through it once, in plain language. This is the moment they realize you're handing them something useful, not selling them something.

End this segment with: *"That's the framework. Let me show you what it looks like in practice."*

### Segment 05 · Three Stories (15 min)

The heart of the meeting. Pick three examples that match the prospect's industry, size, or stated tension. Four to five minutes each. **Try to include one Coach story and one Execute story** so the prospect sees both kinds of work.

**Story structure (consistent across all three):**

1. The company in one sentence (industry, size, what they do)
2. What they were considering or being pitched
3. How the four-question filter played out
4. The decision (skip, reframe, or pilot)
5. What happened next — and what it cost or saved

**Default story bank to draw from:**

- **The HVAC company and the auto-response AI** (Advise / filter outcome). Pitched a customer-trust automation, filtered out as too high-stakes. Customer relationships preserved.
- **The HVAC company and the install-backlog forecast** (Advise / reframe). AI pitch reframed as a regression model in a spreadsheet — 90% of the value at 5% of the cost.
- **The knowledge assistant Claude Project** (Coach). Company SOPs, contracts, and policies loaded into a Claude Project with structured instructions. Two hours of setup. The leadership team can now ask questions about their own documentation. No code, no infrastructure — just configured tooling.
- **The proposal first-drafter** (Coach). Custom GPT trained on past winning proposals + structured prompt = first drafts in minutes that humans edit. Built in an afternoon.
- **The contract-churn signal pipeline** (Execute). Custom-built data pipeline monitoring contract behavior in real time, applying a custom-trained model, surfacing save-the-customer calls. Hosted, integrated, maintained. 7 contracts saved in the first quarter.
- **The auto-Scorecard system** (Execute). Custom integration with the client's specific stack when off-the-shelf tools couldn't bridge the data sources cleanly.
- **The shadow AI conversation** (Advise). Service manager pasting customer addresses into personal ChatGPT. Moved to business tier. 15-minute training. Crisis avoided.

Pick three that map to what the prospect just told you in Segment 02. Keep them concrete — names, numbers, outcomes. Mix tiers so the prospect can see what each kind of work looks like.

### Segment 06 · Engagement Options (15 min)

Walk through the three tiers — Advise, Coach, Execute. Don't pitch — describe. Lead with the Lego framing because it lands fastest.

"Three ways I work with companies. **Advise** is just thinking together — no tools touched, no implementation, just helping the leadership team make better AI decisions. **Coach** is hands-on with existing tools — picking the right Lego pieces and snapping them together. Most AI problems are solvable this way. **Execute** is when we build the new Lego piece that doesn't exist yet — real engineering, custom systems, hosted somewhere your team can use it."

Then walk each in plain detail:

- **Advise.** Outside thinking, no implementation. AI 101, vendor pitch reviews, help thinking through Rocks. ~$1.2–1.5K/month, ~2 hrs/week. Best fit when there's no immediate AI project but pressure is building.
- **Coach.** Configuring existing tools to solve real problems — Claude Projects, Custom GPTs, no-code workflows, vendor SaaS configured to fit. ~$3K/month, ~5 hrs/week. Best fit when the team has small AI initiatives identified and needs someone who knows the tooling landscape to shortcut their evaluation, configuration, and rollout.
- **Execute.** Real engineering work on something novel — custom GTM engines, semantic data agents, integrations no off-the-shelf tool covers. $10–40K per project, 6–12 weeks, fixed-fee, hosted. Best fit when an off-the-shelf tool genuinely can't do it.

Then ask directly: *"Based on what you told me earlier, my honest read is that you're probably an [Advise / Coach / Execute] client. Here's why. Does that match how you're thinking about it?"*

If they're unsure between Coach and Execute, the test is: *"Could a Claude Project, a Custom GPT, or a vendor SaaS configured well do 80% of what you need?"* If yes, Coach. If no, Execute.

If none of the tiers fit, say so. Refer them somewhere useful. That honesty does more for the long game than a forced sale.

### Segment 07 · Open Questions (15 min)

Hand the floor to them. Most prospects have a list of questions they've been holding back. Common ones:

- How is this different from [vendor X they've been pitched]?
- What about [specific tool they've been hearing about]?
- How does this work with our existing implementer?
- What's the realistic ROI?
- How long until we'd see something concrete?
- What does the first 30 days actually look like?

Answer honestly. Don't oversell. If you don't know, say so. The goal is for them to leave thinking *"this person is straight with me"* — that's the hire-able quality.

### Segment 08 · Wrap & Next Steps (5 min)

Close with a clear, low-pressure next step:

"Here's what I'd suggest. I'll send you a one-page summary of what we covered, plus a sample working artifact so you can see what working with me actually looks like. Take a week. If it feels like a fit, we'll set up a working session — that one's paid, and we get into your actual situation. If it doesn't, no hard feelings, and I'll point you toward someone better suited."

Ask one final question: *"Anything else I should know that we didn't cover?"* Sometimes the most important context comes out here.

## Half-Day Format Extension (Optional)

When the prospect is warm and the working component will accelerate the decision, add the following blocks after Segment 06:

### Workshop Block A · Filter a Real Pitch (45 min)

Pick one AI pitch the prospect has actually received. Walk through the four-question filter together, in real time, on their actual situation. They leave with:

- A documented verdict on that specific pitch
- The framework applied to their data and stakes
- A worked example they can re-run on the next pitch themselves

### Workshop Block B · Scope a Potential Rock (45 min)

If they have a candidate AI initiative in mind, walk through the Rock one-pager template:

- Rock statement (binary, measurable)
- Owner (single seat)
- Budget ceiling
- Kill criterion
- Week-6 checkpoint
- Success definition at 90 days

They leave with a draft Rock — not committed to, but visible. The act of scoping reveals whether the work is real or theoretical, and whether it falls into Coach or Execute.

### Lunch / Break (30 min if applicable)

Half-day formats include a meal break. Use it. Conversations during the meal often surface the actual context that matters.

## Post-Meeting Follow-Up

Within 24 hours, send:

- Two-paragraph summary of what was discussed
- The three stories shared, in writing
- The recommended tier and why
- One link to a sample working artifact (Monday Briefing template, Session output, or case study)
- If half-day: the worked example from Workshop Block A or B, cleaned up and presentable
- Clear next step with date — "I'll follow up next Tuesday if I haven't heard from you"

## What This Meeting Is Not

- A working session — we don't solve their AI problems for free (workshop blocks are different — that's structured, time-boxed, and explicitly part of the discovery)
- A pitch deck walkthrough — no slides, no demo
- A scope-and-quote — pricing comes later, in writing, after fit is clear
- A capabilities laundry list — three specific stories beat ten generic ones
- A trial close — no "so what would it take to get started today?" energy

## Notes for Andrew

- Pick the format **before** the meeting is scheduled. Don't show up to a 90-min meeting and try to extend live.
- For Kelly and Dave specifically: half-day format, leadership team in the room, Workshop Block B (Rock scoping) — they're both warm enough that the artifact creates urgency.
- The first 5 minutes set the energy for the rest of the meeting. Frame the meeting honestly and the rest gets easier.
- Resist the urge to fill silence. The best information comes when they're talking, not when you are.
- The Lego framing in Segment 06 is the single sharpest tool for tier qualification. Lead with it.
- If they push for advice on a specific problem outside a workshop block, redirect: *"That's a great question and exactly the kind of thing we'd dig into in a working session — I don't want to give you a half-thought-out answer here."*
- The tiers are a tool to qualify, not a menu to upsell. If they're an Advise client, pitch Advise. Don't reach.
- If a prospect is a clear "no" by minute 30 — wrong size, wrong stage, no operating system — say so kindly and end early. Their time is worth more than a polite full hour.
